A published rate from a national dietary survey of US adults, 2005 to 2010, drinkers and non-drinkers together: days with a drink of 10 grams of alcohol or more, per 100 person-days, with the weekend as the study defined it. Not data from other Tallyist users.
Two denominators, both named. The rate is context for the user’s own split — never a bracket the split falls inside.
Three prose sentences with three different denominators became one table: rows are the source’s own definition of the weekend, columns are whose figure it is. The numeral rule carries the distinction colour is not allowed to — the user’s counts are rounded, the published rates are default SF, so the two kinds of fact read apart without a second hue. Nothing is ranked, nothing is subtracted, and the unit noun is stated once per column rather than once per row.
